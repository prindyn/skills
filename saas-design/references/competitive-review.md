# Competitive Review Guide

A competitive review gives you an objective baseline before redesigning. It reveals industry conventions, competitor weaknesses you can exploit, and design patterns your users already expect. Skipping this step risks building something that either copies competitors too closely or ignores what users have come to expect.

## 1. How to select competitors

Start from the company's business plan or marketing materials — they usually name competitors directly. Aim for 3–5 companies:

- **Direct competitors:** Same product category, same target market
- **Aspirational competitors:** Larger, more established players your prospect might compare you to
- **Adjacent competitors:** Different product but competing for the same budget or attention

For a B2B SaaS startup, choose the companies that a prospect would most likely evaluate side-by-side with your client. These are the most important comparison points.

**Scope the review:** Focus on the home page unless you have specific reasons to look at product or pricing pages. The home page is the primary trust-builder for new visitors.

## 2. Building a site map

Before evaluating anything, map the structure of each competitor's home page. A site map makes the information hierarchy visible and comparable across sites.

For each section of the home page, note:
- What type of content it contains (hero, features, testimonials, etc.)
- Key messages or copy
- Primary CTA in that section

**Example site map entry:**
```
Header
  ├── Nav links (Products, Solutions, Resources, Company)
  └── CTA button: "Schedule a demo"

Hero
  ├── Tagline: "Greater Efficiency, Greater Impact"
  ├── Subtext: 1 sentence
  └── CTA: "See Product Demos"

Section 2: Why us
  ├── Partner values (4 columns with icons)
  └── No CTA

Section 3: Products
  ├── 6-card grid with "Learn More" links
  └── Brief descriptions per product
```

Site maps reveal repetition, missing sections, and structural patterns across competitors.

## 3. The three review lenses

Evaluate every website (including the client's own) across these three lenses:

### Usability
How easy is the site to navigate and understand?

- **Information hierarchy:** Is there a clear, logical order to the sections?
- **Efficiency:** Can a busy user scan and extract key information quickly?
- **Navigation:** Is it easy to find what you're looking for?
- **CTAs:** Are calls-to-action clear, appropriately placed, and non-repetitive?
- **Readability:** Are text blocks an appropriate length? Is the font legible?
- **Section differentiation:** Are sections visually distinct so users don't get disoriented?

Common usability failures in SaaS sites:
- Hero text is too long or dense — users don't read past the first sentence
- CTA appears 4+ times on the page (creates friction for analytical buyers)
- Industry jargon in hero headline alienates non-specialist decision-makers
- "Featured" and "Latest News" sections with duplicate content
- No FAQ or pricing indication, forcing a phone call

### Branding
How does the site communicate the company's identity and values?

- **Voice:** Is the tone consistent? First-person ("we") vs. third-person ("the company") — mixed voice feels unprofessional
- **Key messages:** What themes dominate (efficiency, innovation, reliability, partnership)?
- **Tagline:** Is it memorable and differentiated?
- **Emotional register:** Does the site feel warm and human, or cold and corporate?
- **Audience fit:** Does the brand voice match what the target persona would trust?

Common branding findings across SaaS competitors:
- Most use professional/corporate tone; enthusiastic first-person ("we") stands out positively
- Efficiency, reliability, and innovation dominate messaging — these are table stakes, not differentiators
- Companies that use "we" feel more approachable than those describing themselves in third person
- B2B SaaS brand voices are largely interchangeable — a distinctive voice is a real differentiator

### Visual design
How does the site look, and does it feel cohesive?

- **Color palette:** Is the palette consistent? Primary, secondary, and accent colors clearly defined?
- **Typography:** One or two fonts maximum; consistent sizing and weight hierarchy
- **Image/illustration style:** Stock photos? Custom illustrations? AI-generated? Is it consistent?
- **Visual elements:** Icons, gradients, shapes — do they use a consistent style?
- **Motion:** Are there hover effects, animations, transitions? Do they serve the UX or just look busy?
- **Overall impression:** Does the site look polished and trustworthy, or dated and DIY?

Common visual design patterns in SaaS:
- Blue is the dominant primary color (tech credibility, trust)
- Complementary colors: orange (warmth/energy), green (growth/health), teal (modern/fresh)
- Geometric sans-serif fonts (Poppins, Whitney, Mulish, Inter) are standard
- Abstract shapes and gradients signal "tech company" — used so commonly they no longer differentiate
- AI-generated data imagery looks futuristic but can feel cold
- Mixed visual styles (stock photos + illustrations + icons in different styles) undermine credibility

## 4. Conducting the review

**Format:** Use the worksheet in `templates/competitive-review-table.md`.

**Time per competitor:** 30–60 minutes for a home-page-focused review.

**Process:**
1. Open the live site and capture screenshots of the full page
2. Build the site map from the screenshots
3. Evaluate through each lens, writing brief notes
4. Note specific elements (screenshots, copy) that illustrate each point

**Important:** Review the client's own site using the same process before reviewing competitors. This prevents bias — you approach competitor strengths more objectively when you've already named the client's weaknesses.

## 5. Synthesizing findings

After reviewing all sites, write a comparative synthesis covering:

**Structural patterns:**
- What sections appear on every site? (These are expected by users — don't omit them)
- What sections are absent on most sites that could be a differentiator?
- What is the typical page length? Short (3–5 sections) or long (8+ sections)?

**Branding patterns:**
- What messaging themes dominate the category?
- What tone is used most? What tone is absent?
- What makes each competitor's brand voice distinctive (or not)?

**Visual patterns:**
- What colors dominate? Is there a category convention?
- What image/illustration style is standard? What would stand out?
- What visual techniques are overused? What is notably absent?

**Design recommendations for the client:**
- What competitor weaknesses represent opportunities?
- What do all competitors do well that the client must match?
- What visual direction would differentiate without feeling out of place?

---

## 6. Scoring approach (optional)

For a more structured deliverable, score each site on a 1–5 scale across sub-criteria:

| Criterion | 1 | 5 |
|-----------|---|---|
| Navigation clarity | Confusing, inconsistent | Instantly clear, well-labeled |
| Content efficiency | Dense, hard to skim | Scannable, clear hierarchy |
| Brand voice consistency | Mixed, unclear | Unified, distinctive |
| Visual cohesion | Mismatched styles | Fully consistent design system |
| Social proof | None | Prominent logos, quotes, stats |
| CTA clarity | Buried or repetitive | Clear, well-placed, varied |

Scores are most useful for comparing the client's site against competitors on specific dimensions, not for ranking competitors overall.
