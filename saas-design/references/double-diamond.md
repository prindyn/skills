# Double Diamond Design Process

The Double Diamond is a structured design process developed by the Design Council (UK). It maps the divergent and convergent thinking that happens at each stage of a design project. It is the recommended methodology for SaaS website redesigns because its iterative nature accommodates the reality that websites serve multiple audiences and must balance business goals with user needs.

---

## The four phases

```
     Discover          Define          Develop          Deliver
  ◆────────────◇    ◇────────────◆   ◆────────────◇  ◇────────────◆
  Diverge      Converge           Diverge        Converge
```

Each "diamond" has two phases:
- **Diverge:** Expand your understanding by exploring widely — gather data, generate ideas
- **Converge:** Narrow your focus by synthesizing what you've learned into decisions

The process is non-linear. You may return to Discover after a workshop reveals a gap, or revisit Develop after stakeholder feedback on wireframes. This is expected, not a failure.

---

## Phase 1: Discover

**Purpose:** Understand the problem space before proposing solutions.

**Key activities:**
- Audit the existing website (usability, branding, visual design)
- Competitive review of 3–5 competitor websites
- Secondary research (industry trends, UX conventions)

**Divergent mindset:** Resist the urge to design. Collect information without judgment. The goal is to map what exists and understand the competitive landscape.

**Key outputs:**
- Website audit report with screenshots
- Competitor site maps (structure of each home page)
- Competitive review summary (strengths/weaknesses per competitor)
- List of design opportunities

**Timing (typical):** 2–4 weeks for a focused engagement.

---

## Phase 2: Define

**Purpose:** Synthesize research into a clear problem definition and user understanding.

**Key activities:**
- Proto persona workshop with stakeholders
- User journey mapping
- Synthesis of research findings into design principles

**Convergent mindset:** From everything you've discovered, what matters most? Who is the primary user? What do they need to see, feel, and do on the website?

**Key outputs:**
- Proto persona(s) with demographics, goals, frustrations, personality traits
- User journey map (discovery → interest → consideration → acquisition → loyalty)
- Design brief: the user need the redesign will address, expressed as a clear statement

**Timing:** 1–2 weeks, including a 60–90 minute workshop.

---

## Phase 3: Develop

**Purpose:** Explore and prototype solutions, iterating before committing to a final design.

**Key activities:**
- Site map creation (page structure)
- Low-fidelity wireframes (layout exploration)
- Mood boards (visual direction)
- Visual identity development (color palette, typography)

**Divergent mindset:** Generate multiple options before converging. Create 2–3 mood board directions, explore different wireframe layouts, consider different structural approaches. Cheap exploration here saves expensive revisions later.

**Key outputs:**
- Approved site map
- Low-fidelity wireframes for all key sections
- Selected visual direction from mood boards
- Color palette and typography specification

**Timing:** 2–4 weeks depending on scope.

---

## Phase 4: Deliver

**Purpose:** Produce final design assets that can be implemented.

**Key activities:**
- High-fidelity mockups in Figma (or equivalent)
- Visual identity documentation (brand guidelines)
- Implementation notes for developers
- (Ideally) Usability testing on prototypes

**Convergent mindset:** Stop exploring; start delivering. Decisions should now be locked. Avoid scope creep — note improvement ideas for the next iteration rather than rebuilding.

**Key outputs:**
- High-fidelity mockups for home page (and other key pages if in scope)
- Visual identity document
- Developer handoff notes
- List of future development recommendations

**Timing:** 2–4 weeks.

---

## Applying Double Diamond to a startup context

Startups often have tight timelines and limited resources. Adapt the process accordingly:

| Full process | Lean startup adaptation |
|-------------|------------------------|
| User research interviews | Proto persona workshop (1–2 hours with founders) |
| Usability testing | Informal review with 2–3 people outside the company |
| Full brand guidelines | Color palette + typography + icon style |
| Multiple page designs | Focus on home page first; extend later |
| Iterative prototyping | One round of wireframes → one round of hi-fi |

The spirit of the process matters more than following every step. The core discipline is: **understand before designing, and test before shipping.**

---

## Why it works for website redesigns

Websites serve multiple purposes simultaneously: marketing, trust-building, product education, and conversion. The Double Diamond prevents the common failure mode of designing the site based on internal assumptions ("we know our users") rather than evidence. Each phase forces a different kind of thinking:

- Discover surfaces what's actually happening vs. what is assumed
- Define creates a shared team understanding of who the user is
- Develop allows exploration without immediate commitment
- Deliver ensures that decisions get implemented faithfully

The iterative nature also handles a key reality of SaaS websites: the audience is often narrower and more specialized than founders assume, and what those users need from the site is different from what the founders want to say.
