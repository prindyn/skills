# Good vs. Bad Discovery Questions

Use this file as a sanity check before any customer conversation. Every question you plan to ask should pass the test: **does this question let me learn the truth, or does it invite the person to tell me what I want to hear?**

---

## The Anatomy of a Bad Question

Bad questions share one or more of these traits:

1. **Leading** — they imply the answer you want ("Isn't it frustrating when…?")
2. **Hypothetical** — they ask about the future, not the past ("Would you…?", "Could you see yourself…?")
3. **Idea-first** — they describe your solution before the person has described their problem
4. **Compliment-fishing** — they invite opinion rather than evidence ("What do you think of…?")
5. **Yes/No** — they allow a polite nod instead of information

---

## Side-by-Side Comparison

### Topic: Problem Existence

| Bad | Good |
|---|---|
| "Do you think [problem] is a real issue?" | "Tell me about the last time [problem area] caused you trouble." |
| "Is this something that frustrates you?" | "How do you handle [situation] right now?" |
| "Do people in your role deal with [problem]?" | "Walk me through your typical process for [workflow]." |

---

### Topic: Severity and Frequency

| Bad | Good |
|---|---|
| "Is this a big deal for you?" | "How often does this come up?" |
| "Does this slow you down significantly?" | "What does it cost you when this happens — time, money, customers?" |
| "Is this something you'd prioritize fixing?" | "Have you already tried to fix it? What happened?" |

---

### Topic: Willingness to Pay

| Bad | Good |
|---|---|
| "Would you pay for a solution?" | "What do you currently spend on solving this?" |
| "Would $X/month seem reasonable to you?" | "What tools do you already pay for in this area?" |
| "Do you have budget for this kind of thing?" | "The last time you bought a tool to solve a similar problem, how did that decision get made?" |

---

### Topic: Reaction to Your Idea

| Bad | Good |
|---|---|
| "What do you think of my idea?" | (Don't share the idea yet — learn the problem first.) |
| "Does this sound useful?" | "What would you expect something like this to do for you?" |
| "Would you use this?" | "If something like this existed today, how would you have used it last month?" |

---

### Topic: Current Solutions

| Bad | Good |
|---|---|
| "Are you happy with your current solution?" | "What do you use today to handle this?" |
| "Do you wish your current tool was better?" | "What's the most painful part of your current approach?" |
| "Have you looked at [competitor]?" | "What have you already tried, and why did it fall short?" |

---

### Topic: Commitment Signals

| Bad | Good |
|---|---|
| "Would you be interested in hearing more when we launch?" | "Would you be willing to be one of our first five pilot customers?" |
| "Can I keep you updated?" | "Is there anyone you'd want us to talk to — colleagues who deal with this too?" |
| "Would this be valuable to your team?" | "If we built this and it worked exactly as described, would you be willing to commit to a 3-month pilot?" |

## The "Interesting" Follow-Up Ladder

When someone says something interesting — even vague — pull harder. Don't let threads drop.

**Tier 1 — Open it up:**
- "Can you tell me more about that?"
- "What do you mean by [term they used]?"

**Tier 2 — Ground it in specifics:**
- "Can you give me a specific example?"
- "When did that last happen?"
- "What did you actually do?"

**Tier 3 — Pull on the emotion:**
- "How did that feel?"
- "What was the cost — financial, time, relationship?"
- "Did you try to fix it? What happened?"

**Tier 4 — Test the priority:**
- "If you could wave a magic wand and fix one thing in this area, what would it be?"
- "Is this the biggest pain point in [workflow], or is something else worse?"
- "How high on your priority list is solving this?"

## Questions About the Person, Not the Problem

These are useful warm-up questions that build context and rapport without revealing your idea:

- "What's your role, and what does a typical week look like for you?"
- "What's the hardest part of your job right now?"
- "What's something you wish you could spend more time on — but can't because [x] gets in the way?"
- "What tools or systems do you rely on most heavily?"
- "What's broken that you've just accepted as broken?"

---

## Questions to Never Ask (and What to Ask Instead)

| Never ask | Ask instead |
|---|---|
| "Do you like this idea?" | Don't share the idea during discovery. |
| "Would you recommend this to a friend?" | "Who else do you know who deals with this kind of problem?" |
| "Is the problem serious enough to justify a product?" | "What have you done to try to solve this on your own?" |
| "Are you our target customer?" | "Who else at your company deals with this? How do decisions like this get made?" |
| "What features would you want?" | "What was the last workaround you built? What did it take?" |
