# Interview Script Template

Use this as a flexible guide, not a rigid script. Customer conversations should feel like a curious, casual chat — not a survey. Adapt based on where the person takes you.

**Before the call:** Run your question list through `agents/question-critic.md`. Remove or rewrite any leading, hypothetical, or compliment-fishing questions.

**During the call:** Take notes in `templates/interview-notes.md`. Keep your phone or laptop handy, but make it feel secondary.

---

## Opening (2–3 minutes)

> Goal: put them at ease, set expectations, and position yourself as a learner, not a seller.

Suggested language:

> "Thanks so much for making time. I'm [name], and I'm working on [brief, vague framing — e.g., 'exploring tools for operations teams']. I'm not going to pitch you anything today — I'm honestly just trying to understand how people like you handle [problem area]. Your experience is way more valuable to me than any answer I could give you. I'll ask a few questions, and I'd love if you just talk freely. Sound good?"

---

## Warm-Up Questions (3–5 minutes)

> Goal: understand their context and day-to-day before zooming in.

Questions to pick from (choose 2–3 that fit your context):

1. "What does your role actually involve day-to-day? What takes up most of your time?"
2. "What tools and systems do you rely on most heavily?"
3. "What's the hardest part of your job right now?"
4. "If you could wave a magic wand and fix one thing about how [workflow area] works, what would it be?"
5. "What's something you've just accepted as broken — that nobody's fixed yet?"

> **Listen for:** mentions of your problem space, tools they use, frustrations. Note anything worth digging into. Don't redirect yet — just absorb.

---

## Core Discovery Questions (15–20 minutes)

> Goal: understand if the problem is real, how painful it is, and what they currently do about it.

Replace [PROBLEM AREA] with your specific topic throughout.

**Opening the problem space:**
```
1. "Tell me about the last time [PROBLEM AREA] came up for you. Walk me through what actually happened."

2. "How do you handle [PROBLEM AREA] today? What's the process?"
```

**Probing frequency and severity:**
```
3. "How often does this come up?"

4. "What happens when it goes wrong? What's the actual cost — in time, money, or stress?"
```

**Understanding current behavior:**
```
5. "What have you tried so far to solve or manage this? What's your current workaround?"

6. "Why does [current workaround] fall short? What's the most frustrating part of it?"
```

**Probing existing spend:**
```
7. "Have you ever paid for something to help with this — a tool, a consultant, a service? How much?"
```

**Surfacing priority:**
```
8. "If you could only fix one thing in [PROBLEM AREA], what would it be?"

9. "How high on your list of priorities is fixing this, compared to other things you're working on?"
```

> **Follow-up ladder (use often):**
> - "Can you say more about that?"
> - "Can you give me a specific example?"
> - "When did that happen last?"
> - "What did you actually do in that moment?"
> - "How did that feel?"

---

## Closing Questions (3–5 minutes)

> Goal: surface referrals and test for commitment.

**Referrals:**
```
"Who else do you know who deals with this kind of thing? Would you be willing to make an intro?"
```

**Testing interest / commitment:**
```
"We're putting together a small group of early testers. Would you be open to being part of that? It'd be [time expectation] of your time."
```

**The wrap:**
```
"This has been really helpful. Is there anything you think I should have asked but didn't — anything important about this problem that I might be missing?"
```

---

## Closing the Conversation

> After the conversation, **never pitch immediately**. Thank them, confirm any next steps, and leave.

If they ask "so what are you building?":

> "I'm still figuring that out honestly — I want to make sure I really understand the problem before designing any solution. But I'd love to keep you in the loop as we learn more. What's the best way to reach you?"

If you've learned everything you need and you want to share your idea to test reaction:

> Frame it lightly: "Would it be OK if I shared a rough direction we're exploring and got your quick reaction? I'd take it with a grain of salt, but I'm curious what you'd say."
> Then watch how quickly the conversation shifts from their reality to your pitch. That's useful information.
